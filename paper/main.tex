\begin{abstract}
A scene reconstructed from video may later be physically edited: stiffness, damping, or other parameters are changed and the updated model is simulated under new interactions. A lower held-out fitting error does not guarantee that such an edit improves the physics that matters after deployment or authoring. Reality Probe addresses this acceptance problem by testing a proposed physical state against measurements withheld from fitting and returning SUPPORT, VETO, or UNRESOLVED before the state is committed. Across controlled and measured studies, the main finding is that verification quality depends on the information carried by the physical test; numerically sound probes can remain uninformative, while a dynamics-matched test can separate competing models. On a locked 10-video IRIS pendulum evaluation, the matched probe reaches 81.8\% decision accuracy versus 36.4\% for a small-angle baseline; a separately frozen free-fall protocol fails untouched validation and is not promoted to final testing. Reality Probe therefore turns physical editing from fit-and-commit into fit, test, then commit, making unsupported state changes explicit instead of silently folding them into the reconstructed world.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Suppose a cloth has been reconstructed from video and assigned a simulated stiffness. A later optimization pass proposes a new stiffness because it lowers trajectory error on held-out frames. If the system simply overwrites the old value, it is treating better fit as evidence of better physics. Those are different claims. The new parameter may reproduce the recorded motion more closely and still respond worse when the cloth is pulled, struck, or loaded in a way that was never part of fitting.

This problem appears whenever a reconstructed scene is expected to remain physically editable. By a \emph{video-reconstructed world}, we mean a scene model recovered from observations that stores geometry or appearance together with state or parameters used by a simulator. Physics-aware radiance fields, Gaussian representations, and differentiable simulators combine reconstruction, parameter estimation, and interaction synthesis \citep{li2023pacnerf,xie2024physgaussian,zhang2024physdreamer,liu2025physflow,rho2026monophysics,wada2026mphygs,hu2019chainqueen,hu2020difftaichi}. Once such a model persists beyond the fitting run, every physical parameter change becomes a state-management decision: should the system keep the old model or commit the new one?

Inverse physics usually answers a different question: which parameters best explain the observations used for estimation? Structural identifiability asks whether those observations can determine the parameters at all, and experiment design asks which measurements make competing models easier to distinguish \citep{wang2026identifiability,asadi2025oed}. Verification and validation ask whether a computational model is credible for an intended use \citep{oberkampf2007pcmm,laudy2026digitaltwin}. The missing systems step is the acceptance decision for one concrete physical edit after an optimizer has proposed it. A persistent scene needs a way to say: \emph{this change is physically supported}, \emph{this change is contradicted}, or \emph{the available test does not tell us enough}.

Reality Probe inserts that decision between proposal and commit. Fitting produces a provisional model from training and ordinary held-out observations. Once that model is fixed, a reserved physical observation or intervention is evaluated against both the current and proposed states. Measurement, repeat, and numerical uncertainty are carried into the comparison. The result is SUPPORT, VETO, or UNRESOLVED. SUPPORT permits a verified commit; VETO keeps the current state because the proposal is physically worse on the reserved test; UNRESOLVED also preserves the current state, but for a different reason: the experiment cannot separate the two models strongly enough.

Figure~\ref{fig:overview} shows the failure that motivates this gate. In one frozen synthetic example, the proposed state improves ordinary held-out error by 14.93\% but worsens an untouched physical target by 9.03\%. The example is illustrative and carries no special statistical weight. What matters is the structure of the failure: the metric used to choose the edit and the physical behavior we ultimately care about can move in opposite directions.

The central hypothesis of this paper is not that one verifier works everywhere. It is that the usefulness of verification depends on whether the reserved physical test contains information that distinguishes the proposed state from the current one. We therefore evaluate several physical tests and do not tune one mechanism until it passes. Dark-Field Counterfactual Spectroscopy (DCS) tests whether signed intervention contrasts expose higher-order disagreement. Known-force compliance changes the physical question entirely. IRIS pendulum video provides a measured setting where a dynamics-matched observable succeeds. A separately frozen free-fall protocol provides a measured setting where the verification approach fails validation. The negative result is part of the contribution because it shows where the acceptance gate must refuse to generalize.

The impact is architectural. A physically editable scene should not be a fit-and-commit pipeline. It should be a \emph{fit, test, then commit} pipeline in which the acceptance measurements are separated from the data that produced the proposal. This makes silent physical regressions observable and gives the system a principled reason to preserve the previous state when its experiment is uninformative.

The paper makes three contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\item
  \textbf{An evidence-gated commit model for physical scene edits.} Reality Probe separates proposal data from acceptance data, propagates the uncertainty of the reserved test, and records SUPPORT, VETO, or UNRESOLVED before a proposed physical state can replace the baseline.
\item
  \textbf{An empirical study of verification information.} Controlled synthetic experiments show that mathematically valid and numerically stable probes can remain too weak to support a decision, while changing the physical intervention can increase separability substantially.
\item
  \textbf{A locked measured-video evaluation with both success and failure.} A pendulum-specific probe improves decision accuracy on fresh IRIS video, while a separately frozen free-fall protocol fails untouched validation. Together they bound the claim to evidence-matched physical tests.
\end{enumerate}

The rest of the paper asks a single question: \emph{what evidence justifies committing a physical edit to a persistent reconstructed world?}

\section{Related Work}\label{related-work}

\subsection{Physics-aware scene reconstruction and inverse physics}\label{physics-aware-scene-representations-and-inverse-physics}

PAC-NeRF couples continuum simulation to a neural radiance field so that physical parameters can be estimated from multi-view video \citep{li2023pacnerf}. PhysGaussian brings Material Point Method dynamics to 3D Gaussian primitives \citep{xie2024physgaussian}. PhysDreamer, PhysFlow, MonoPhysics, and M-PhyGs extend physically parameterized visual representations toward interaction synthesis, material estimation, and monocular or multi-material dynamics \citep{zhang2024physdreamer,liu2025physflow,rho2026monophysics,wada2026mphygs}. Differentiable simulators such as ChainQueen and DiffTaichi provide optimization machinery for physical parameters \citep{hu2019chainqueen,hu2020difftaichi}.

These systems motivate our setting: a scene model can carry physical state that is estimated from observations and later changed. Reality Probe addresses the next operation in that lifecycle. Given a baseline model and a proposed physical edit, should the persistent scene accept the new state?

\subsection{Identifiability and informative experiments}\label{identifiability-and-experimental-design}

Parameter recovery is limited by the information present in the observations. Structural identifiability formalizes whether the model parameters are uniquely recoverable, and recent work studies these conditions directly from video \citep{wang2026identifiability}. IRIS evaluates physical parameter recovery, identifiability, extrapolation, and governing-equation recovery on controlled real video \citep{khanbayov2026iris}. Optimal experiment design uses sensitivity or information measures to choose loading conditions that distinguish competing material models \citep{asadi2025oed}.

Reality Probe uses the same information principle for a different decision. The proposal already exists. The question is whether a reserved experiment separates it from the current model strongly enough to justify changing persistent state. An unresolved outcome therefore says something about the experiment: the evidence available for acceptance is insufficient, even if parameter fitting itself converged.

\subsection{Model credibility, physical fidelity, and abstention}\label{simulation-credibility-and-measured-physical-fidelity}

Verification, validation, and uncertainty quantification separate numerical correctness from model credibility for a stated use \citep{oberkampf2007pcmm}. Digital-twin research similarly asks how simulated counterfactuals should be validated \citep{laudy2026digitaltwin}. GAUGE grounds physical-fidelity comparisons in measured motion across several mechanical task families \citep{wang2026gauge}. These lines of work establish that fit alone is not enough to establish physical credibility.

The three-way decision also has clear prior art. Reject-option analysis studies the error--rejection tradeoff \citep{chow1970reject}, and selective prediction learns mechanisms that abstain when a prediction is not sufficiently reliable \citep{geifman2019selectivenet}. We do not claim abstention as a new decision primitive. The contribution is where it is placed: UNRESOLVED is a first-class state of a physical-edit transaction, so an uninformative experiment prevents a proposed model from silently replacing the current one.

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{problem-formulation}

Let \(M_0\) be the current physical model stored in a video-reconstructed world and \(M_1\) a proposed edit produced by fitting or optimization. We separate observations into three roles:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{fitting evidence}, which may change model parameters or generate \(M_1\);
\item \textbf{ordinary held-out evidence}, which measures generalization within the same observation family used for fitting; and
\item \textbf{reserved physical evidence}, which is withheld from proposal selection and used only to decide whether \(M_1\) should replace \(M_0\).
\end{itemize}

Let \(L_{\mathrm{obs}}(M)\) denote ordinary held-out error and \(L_{\mathrm{phys}}(M)\) error on an untouched physical target, with lower values preferred. A \emph{fit-improving physical regression} occurs when

\[
L_{\mathrm{obs}}(M_1) < L_{\mathrm{obs}}(M_0)
\quad\text{but}\quad
L_{\mathrm{phys}}(M_1) > L_{\mathrm{phys}}(M_0).
\]

This definition is intentionally agnostic to cause. Parameter compensation, model-form error, boundary assumptions, numerical approximations, and weakly informative observations can all produce the same evidence pattern.

For acceptance, let \(Y\) be the reserved physical evidence and \(e(M,Y)\) the error of model \(M\) on that evidence. We compare the proposed and current models using

\[
z =
\frac{e(M_0,Y)-e(M_1,Y)}
     {\sigma},
\]

where \(\sigma\) is the uncertainty scale of the comparison. Positive \(z\) favors the proposal; negative \(z\) favors the current model. Dividing by \(\sigma\) asks whether the observed difference is large relative to measurement, repeat, and numerical uncertainty. We use \(z\) as a standardized evidence score, not as a Gaussian hypothesis-test statistic.

The experiments use the repository-frozen decision rule

\[
\begin{aligned}
z \geq 2 &\Rightarrow \mathrm{SUPPORT},\\
z \leq -2 &\Rightarrow \mathrm{VETO},\\
|z|<2 &\Rightarrow \mathrm{UNRESOLVED}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The magnitude 2 is an operational threshold for this study, not a universal physical-credibility constant. Keeping it fixed after data access is what makes a negative result meaningful: weak evidence remains weak instead of becoming a decision through threshold relaxation.

\section{Reality Probe}\label{reality-probe}

\subsection{From proposal to commit}\label{captured-world-rewrite-and-verification-pipeline}

Reality Probe is a commit gate around an existing physical-model update process. The optimizer is free to use its fitting objective to produce \(M_1\). Once the proposal is fixed, the acceptance stage evaluates a reserved physical test against both \(M_0\) and \(M_1\). The resulting evidence score and uncertainty are stored with provenance before any state change occurs.

SUPPORT means \(M_1\) clears the positive evidence margin and may replace the baseline. VETO means the reserved experiment favors \(M_0\) strongly enough to reject the proposal. UNRESOLVED means the experiment does not separate them; the system therefore preserves \(M_0\) without pretending that \(M_1\) has been disproved. This distinction matters operationally because contradiction and lack of information should not be recorded as the same event.

The separation is causal as well as organizational. If the reserved measurement is consulted while choosing \(M_1\), it stops being acceptance evidence and becomes another fitting signal. Reality Probe therefore treats proposal generation and acceptance as sequential transactions with separate provenance.

\subsection{A mechanism-selective probe: DCS}\label{dark-field-counterfactual-spectroscopy}

Dark-Field Counterfactual Spectroscopy is one way to construct reserved evidence when several controlled interventions are available. For physical model \(M\) and intervention \(u\), let \(F_M(u)\) be the measured response. DCS forms a signed combination

\[
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}[F_M]
=
\sum_i w_i F_M(u_i),
\]

with weights chosen so that low-order response terms cancel:

\[
r_\alpha
=
\sum_i w_i u_i^\alpha
\approx 0,
\qquad
|\alpha|<k.
\]

Here \(\alpha\) indexes a monomial in the intervention coordinates and \(k\) is the requested cancellation order. The purpose is not to invent a new finite-difference construction; it is to suppress response shared by competing models so that disagreement in higher-order behavior becomes easier to observe.

For intuition, the one-dimensional second-order contrast

\begin{equation}\label{eq:second}
F(+a)+F(-a)-2F(0)
\end{equation}

removes the constant term and cancels a response that is linear in intervention amplitude \(a\). The reported experiments synthesize the actual order-2 comparator on a \(3\times3\) intervention lattice instead of using Eq.~\ref{eq:second} directly.

The implementation also evaluates the Boolean-lattice Möbius contrast

\[
\kappa(S)
=
\sum_{T\subseteq S}
(-1)^{|S|-|T|}F(T),
\]

which removes contributions attributable to lower-order subsets of intervention factors. We use it as an interaction statistic; the underlying inclusion--exclusion construction is classical.

\subsection{Uncertainty and the acceptance score}\label{uncertainty-and-standardized-separation}

The acceptance score is only useful if the uncertainty denominator refers to the same physical quantity as the numerator. We therefore track measurement uncertainty, variation across repeated observations, and numerical sensitivity to solver resolution. Signed DCS combinations propagate independent measurement and repeat variance through the squared weight gain \(\sum_i w_i^2\). Numerical error can be correlated across intervention points, so the refinement experiments measure the same witness at nominal and finer solver resolution instead of assuming independence.

For witness selection, active DCS synthesis searches the nullspace of the lower-moment constraints and evaluates deterministic projected directions. The resulting maximin search is non-convex, so we treat the selected witness as a reproducible heuristic and make no global-optimality claim.

The important systems property is independent of the particular probe: the same acceptance interface can consume a DCS witness, a force-compliance response, a pendulum period residual, or another reserved physical measurement. What changes is the information carried by \(Y\), not the semantics of SUPPORT, VETO, and UNRESOLVED.

\subsection{Controlled study of probe informativeness}\label{synthetic-falsification-ladder}

The synthetic study begins with a deliberately strong positive control (D1): 64 parameter changes improve the ordinary fitting criterion while degrading an untouched physical target. This stage tests the DCS implementation, not generalization.

The next two stages freeze the selected probe and move to unseen truth worlds. D2 tests whether the signed witness reaches the fixed decision margin on 16 new worlds. D3 repeats the comparison on six fresh worlds while evaluating the same witness at finer numerical resolution, allowing us to distinguish weak physical separation from simple solver error.

The final synthetic verification stage (D4V) starts from 36 edits that all improve ordinary held-out fit. Their untouched physical behavior separates them into 14 regressive edits and 22 beneficial edits. DCS is compared with raw-bundle, pair-specific raw, Fisher/Jacobian, and maximum-motion alternatives under the same \(|z|\ge2\) decision rule.

\subsection{Changing the physical question}\label{fresh-orthogonal-force-compliance-study}

The force-compliance study holds proposal generation fixed and changes only the physical test. Fresh prospective force evidence is synthetic in this paper; no measured force-sensor claim is made. Six APIC truth worlds span three Young's moduli and two Poisson ratios. Each proposal is evaluated under four known load directions (\(+x\), \(-x\), \(+y\), and \(+z\)); the response records top-layer and interior displacement.

The same decision magnitude \(\tau=2\) is used for DCS and force compliance. Numerical uncertainty is measured by repeating the proposed-model response at half timestep, while measurement and repeat scales are fixed before execution. The purpose of this study is not to rescue DCS with a different threshold. It asks whether a physically different experiment makes the current and proposed models easier to distinguish.

\subsection{Locked real-video evaluation}\label{locked-iris-pendulum-protocol}

The positive measured test uses the IRIS \texttt{pendulum\_90} setting. Before the ten final videos are opened, the tracker, proposal schedule, physical probe, and decision rule are frozen. The primary test uses the finite-amplitude pendulum period relation; the diagnostic baseline uses the small-angle approximation. Each physical video produces 11 controlled SUPPORT/VETO/UNRESOLVED cases, giving 110 nested case rows across ten videos.

A separate free-fall protocol tests transfer to another equation family. Development uses \texttt{drop\_50/06..10}; validation uses untouched \texttt{drop\_100/06..10}. The designated final set \texttt{drop\_150/02..10} is opened only if validation passes. Thresholds, tracker logic, proposal selection, and scene membership are frozen before validation.

Post-final pendulum analyses are kept separate from the locked result. They cluster by physical video, compare additional residual models, generate proposals without ground-truth access, vary proposal/probe overlap, propagate rope-length uncertainty, and stress the video pipeline under corruption and temporal downsampling.

\section{Results}\label{results-and-analysis}

\subsection{Better fit is not a physical acceptance test}\label{deceptive-repair-is-a-distinct-failure-mode}

Fit-improving physical regressions occur in both controlled proposal populations. Fourteen of 36 D4V edits and 12 of 36 fresh force-study edits improve ordinary held-out error while worsening an untouched physical target. These fractions describe the constructed test populations; they are not prevalence estimates.

The consequence is more important than the count. A pipeline that commits an edit because held-out trajectory error improved would accept physically worse states in these examples. Figure~\ref{fig:deceptive-repair} shows one case in which ordinary error falls by 14.93\% while untouched-target error rises by 9.03\%.

\subsection{Probe sophistication cannot compensate for an uninformative experiment}\label{the-dcs-hypothesis-is-falsified-as-a-general-prospective-verifier}

DCS passes the constructed control: all 64 deliberately regressive parameter changes are rejected. That result verifies the implementation and confirms that signed intervention contrasts can expose a strong difference when one is present.

The frozen synthetic evaluations tell a different story. On unseen truth worlds, DCS remains far below the acceptance margin. Numerical refinement reduces solver discrepancy but does not materially increase separation. In the D4V proposal population, DCS, raw bundles, pair-specific raw responses, Fisher/Jacobian selection, and maximum-motion selection all resolve 0/36 proposals at \(|z|\ge2\). DCS is therefore neither uniquely failing nor uniquely superior: the kinematic experiment itself does not provide enough evidence for the acceptance decision.

GAUGE shows the same distinction on measured data. In the retrospective shearing study, ordinary marker error prefers the finite-overlap model, while the longitudinal mechanism channel prefers the endpoint model in 9/10 held-out repeats. In the separately frozen validation study, the raw truth direction is correct but the witness signal is tiny relative to repeat and numerical variability; directional SUPPORT/VETO coverage is 0/24, while all 12 placebo cases correctly remain UNRESOLVED.

Changing the physical question has a much larger effect. Known-force compliance raises median standardized evidence magnitude from 0.0488 to 0.5586 relative to fresh DCS, an 11.46\(\times\) increase, although no proposal crosses the frozen decision boundary. Table~\ref{tab:ofc} summarizes the result.

The physical video, not the case row, is the independent unit. The primary probe beats the baseline on all ten videos; an exact two-sided sign test over those ten paired video outcomes gives \(p=0.001953125\). Two stronger post-final residual baselines also remain below the locked primary result.

The success is not an artifact of manually scheduled parameter factors. In a post-final GT-hidden test, proposals are generated from an early video segment and committed to a hash before ground truth is joined; all ten ordinary-improving proposals are then classified correctly on reserved evidence. The decision also remains stable when proposal and probe windows are varied from disjoint to fully overlapping, although both windows still come from the same sensor stream.

Robustness tests identify a concrete acquisition boundary. Noise, blur, modest frame duplication/drop, central occlusion, and crop perturbations preserve the locked 81.82\% accuracy in the tested ranges. Temporal downsampling does not: accuracy falls to 63.64\% at 30 fps and 45.45\% at 15 fps. The verifier therefore depends on preserving the temporal information required by the pendulum observable.

\subsection{Transfer to free-fall fails, so the claim stays conditional}\label{second-domain-iris-freefall-failure}

The free-fall protocol passes development before untouched validation is opened. On \texttt{drop\_50}, four of five videos pass quality and the median acceleration relative error is 8.05\%.

The same frozen logic fails on \texttt{drop\_100/06..10}. All five videos pass low-level quality checks, yet median acceleration relative error rises to 111.7\%. The selected intervals are consistent with a locally ballistic partial trajectory rather than the full known-height fall. Post-validation diagnostics explore scaling, endpoint anchors, track stitching, resolution, association, ranking, and bidirectional reconstruction, but none yields a robust target-free correction across the spent development and validation evidence.

The protocol therefore stops. \texttt{drop\_100} becomes retrospective evidence, \texttt{drop\_150/02..10} remains unopened, and the paper does not convert this failure into a second positive result. The implication is direct: Reality Probe does not remove the need to design a valid physical test. It makes a failed test visible before that evidence is used to approve a model change.

\section{Discussion}\label{discussion}

\subsection{From fit-and-commit to test-and-commit}\label{why-the-channels-behave-differently}

The main systems consequence is a change in where physical validation sits. In a conventional fitting loop, a lower held-out loss may be enough to accept new parameters. Reality Probe inserts an explicit acceptance stage after fitting. The proposed state remains provisional until a reserved physical observation or intervention supports it.

This matters for any application that keeps a physically parameterized scene alive after reconstruction. A graphics authoring system may update material behavior; a simulation-backed digital twin may recalibrate a parameter; a robotics world model may refine contact or compliance. The paper does not claim deployment validation for all of these domains. The architectural lesson is narrower and transferable: persistent physical state should not be overwritten solely because the fitting objective improved.

The three-way decision also changes failure handling. VETO means the reserved test contradicts the proposed state. UNRESOLVED means the experiment is not informative enough to decide. Keeping these states separate prevents an evidence-poor test from masquerading as either success or failure.

\subsection{Verification is also an experiment-design problem}\label{relation-to-identifiability-and-experiment-design}

The contrast between DCS, force compliance, pendulum, and free-fall connects the acceptance problem to identifiability and experiment design \citep{wang2026identifiability,asadi2025oed}. The same two physical models can be almost indistinguishable under one observation and easy to separate under another. In our experiments, numerical refinement changes the evidence less than changing the physical channel.

This suggests a practical design loop for future systems. When the verifier returns UNRESOLVED, the next action should not automatically be a lower threshold or a stronger optimizer. The system should ask which intervention, sensor, or time scale is most likely to separate the competing physical states. Reality Probe provides the transaction boundary; informative experiment selection determines whether that boundary can be crossed.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}\label{limitations}

The strongest measured positive result covers one IRIS pendulum regime and ten physical videos. The 110 controlled rows are nested within those ten videos, so the physical sample size is ten. The post-final clustered analysis respects that structure, but broader claims require different pendulum lengths, cameras, amplitudes, and capture conditions.

The force-compliance result is synthetic. It establishes that a different physical question can increase separability under controlled ground truth; it does not establish measured force-sensor verification. GAUGE provides measured evidence but its frozen directional validation remains unresolved.

Temporal resolution is a concrete acquisition limit. The pendulum decision degrades sharply under downsampling. A deployment using this observable would therefore need an acquisition rate that preserves the relevant period information.

The free-fall failure bounds cross-domain generality. The development result does not survive untouched validation, and the final split remains unopened. Reality Probe cannot compensate for an event definition that does not correspond to the physical quantity used by the estimator.

The synthetic mechanics experiments also cover a limited family of MPM configurations. DCS witness selection is deterministic but heuristic, and the decision magnitude of 2 is a frozen operational rule for this study and has no claim to universal validity.

Finally, proposal and verification windows in the pendulum study come from the same video stream. Their temporal overlap can be changed, but they are not independent sensor modalities. A stronger future test would reserve a separately instrumented physical channel.

\subsection{Implications for future physically editable scene systems}\label{future-work}

The most useful next experiment is not another threshold sweep. It is a measured intervention whose information content is chosen before final data access: a calibrated external force, modal excitation, or another sensor-backed response that targets the physical parameter being edited. The proposal-generation pipeline should remain blind to that reserved channel.

At the system level, the acceptance record should persist with the model state: which proposal was made, which measurement was withheld from fitting, what uncertainty was assigned, what decision was reached, and which version was committed or preserved. This turns physical editing into an auditable state transition instead of an opaque optimizer update.

\section{Reproducibility and Implementation Notes}\label{reproducibility-and-implementation-notes}

The repository records each frozen experiment with code revision, dataset revision, protocol files, machine-readable result ledgers, and SHA-256 manifests. The core synthetic study, locked pendulum final test, and failed free-fall validation are tied to separate commits so that later analysis cannot silently redefine an earlier gate. Full immutable identifiers and exact commands remain in the result ledgers, keeping the manuscript readable without weakening provenance.

The C++20 implementation includes nonlinear MPM replay, APIC/PIC transfer variants, finite-difference and adjoint influence paths, DCS moment checks, uncertainty propagation, witness synthesis, numerical refinement, and commit/rollback bookkeeping. Experiment drivers are kept separate from the core implementation, allowing a validation failure to remain frozen while later diagnostic code evolves.

The locked pendulum result was also replayed on Ubuntu with current dependencies. All discrete final decisions reproduce exactly, including the primary/baseline correctness counts and paired outcomes. The median period-inferred length error changes by about 0.10\% relative between the locked Mac/Metal run and Ubuntu. We therefore claim decision-level cross-platform reproduction, not bitwise numerical identity.

Paper figures are generated from committed trajectories or result ledgers. Display magnification is disclosed where used and never changes the reported numerical values. The Stanford Bunny is a visualization carrier, not benchmark geometry.

\paragraph{Generative-AI-assisted research workflow.}
OpenAI ChatGPT was used for code drafting and debugging, experiment orchestration and analysis scripts, visualization/manuscript tooling, and manuscript revision. AI-generated text or code was not treated as empirical evidence. Numerical claims were checked against committed result artifacts, frozen protocol state, and cited publication records.

\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

A physically editable scene has two separate problems: estimating a better model and deciding whether that model should replace the one already stored. This paper focuses on the second problem. Reality Probe places a reserved physical test between proposal and commit, so a proposal can be accepted, rejected, or left pending when the experiment is not informative enough.

The experiments show why that separation matters. Better held-out fit can coincide with worse physical behavior. Numerically correct probes can still fail because the chosen observation does not distinguish the competing models. Changing the experiment can increase separability far more than refining the same estimator. A dynamics-matched pendulum test succeeds on fresh measured video, while a separately frozen free-fall protocol fails validation and is stopped; the validation set is not recycled into development.

The practical implication is a change in system architecture. Video-reconstructed worlds that carry persistent physical state should not let optimization overwrite that state automatically. They should treat a physical edit as provisional until reserved evidence supports it, preserve the previous model when evidence is weak, and record why the transition was or was not committed. Reality Probe provides that acceptance boundary; designing informative physical tests determines how often the system can cross it.
