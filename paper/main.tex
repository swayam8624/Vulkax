\begin{abstract}
Captured scenes are increasingly used as executable worlds whose physical parameters can be fitted, edited, and replayed. A lower held-out observation error, however, does not by itself establish that a physical edit is more correct. We study this failure mode as deceptive physical repair: a candidate rewrite improves ordinary held-out agreement while worsening an untouched physical target. We introduce Reality Probe, an evidence-governed verification framework that separates proposal generation from independent physical evidence and permits support, veto, or unresolved outcomes. Reality Probe is the framework; Dark-Field Counterfactual Spectroscopy (DCS) is one mechanism-selective instrument tested within it. DCS constructs signed intervention contrasts that cancel lower-order common response and evaluates the residual under measurement, repeat, and numerical uncertainty. A staged falsification program shows that DCS succeeds on constructed controls but does not provide useful prospective repair-verification coverage in frozen validation: D2 resolves 0/16 truth worlds, D3 resolves 0/6, and D4V resolves 0/36 ordinary-improving repair proposals, including 14 deceptive repairs. We then freeze a new known-force compliance experiment on fresh synthetic truth worlds. This orthogonal physical channel increases median standardized evidence magnitude from 0.0488 to 0.5586, an 11.46$\times$ gain, yet its maximum magnitude is 1.3191 against the frozen decision magnitude of 2, leaving all 36 proposals unresolved. A retrospective measured GAUGE study independently exhibits channel disagreement. These results support a narrower conclusion than a universal verifier: physical verification in the tested captured-world regimes can be information-limited, and a trustworthy system should refuse certification when the available interventions do not provide sufficient discriminating evidence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Recent systems increasingly attach physical state to visual scene
representations so that captured objects can be simulated, edited, and
interrogated under new interactions. Physics-aware neural fields and
Gaussian representations have made this coupling practical across
dynamic reconstruction, material inference, and interaction synthesis
\citep{li2023pacnerf,xie2024physgaussian,zhang2024physdreamer,liu2025physflow,rho2026monophysics,wada2026mphygs}. These systems raise a
verification question that is distinct from fitting: after a candidate
physical rewrite improves the observations used to assess it, what
evidence establishes that the rewrite is actually more correct under
interventions that were not part of the repair objective?

This distinction matters because inverse physical models are commonly
underdetermined by the observations available to them. Different
parameterizations, transfer schemes, boundary assumptions, numerical
settings, or constitutive approximations can fit similar observed motion
while making different predictions elsewhere. Work on structural
identifiability, optimal experimental design, model discrepancy, and
simulation credibility already provides mature tools for analyzing
related ambiguity \citep{wang2026identifiability,asadi2025oed,oberkampf2007pcmm,laudy2026digitaltwin}. Our focus is narrower
and operational: the object being judged is a \emph{requested physical
rewrite} of a persistent captured world, and the verifier must decide
whether independent evidence supports the rewrite, contradicts it, or is
insufficient to decide.

We call a repair \emph{deceptive} when it improves an ordinary held-out
observation metric while worsening an untouched physical target. This is
not a claim about intent or a particular optimizer. It is a failure mode
of the evidence used to approve an edit. The selected case below shows
one exact example from our fresh synthetic force-compliance experiment. The repair
reduces ordinary held-out error by 14.93\% while increasing the
untouched-target error by 9.03\%. The case is selected only for
visualization; aggregate statistics and decision thresholds are
independent of this selection.

We investigate this problem through \emph{Reality Probe}, an
evidence-governed verification pipeline for captured executable worlds.
Candidate repairs are proposed using ordinary calibration and held-out
observations. Verification then applies controlled physical
interventions that were not used to choose the repair, compares the
truth-like reference response with candidate responses, propagates
measurement, repeat, and numerical uncertainty, and produces a support,
veto, or unresolved decision. Reality Probe is the contribution-level framework: it separates
proposal generation from evidence, carries explicit uncertainty,
preserves support/veto/unresolved outcomes, quantifies the information
frontier, and permits refusal. Dark-Field Counterfactual Spectroscopy
(DCS) is one mechanism-selective instrument tested inside that framework.
DCS constructs signed intervention contrasts that cancel lower-order
common response so that residual disagreement becomes more visible. The
operators used to build these contrasts are classical finite-difference
and inclusion-exclusion constructions; our contribution is their use as
a repair-verification instrument and, equally importantly, the empirical
characterization of where that instrument fails.

The paper follows a falsification sequence rather than a positive-only
benchmark. DCS first passes a constructed positive control. It then
fails to produce resolved evidence on frozen truth-world validation,
adaptive witness-space refinement, and a pair-specific repair-veto
population. Matched raw, Fisher-style, and maximum-motion comparators
likewise provide zero resolved coverage in the repair-veto population.
We therefore quantify the \emph{information frontier}: how far the
observed standardized evidence lies from the frozen decision reference.
Finally, we freeze a new orthogonal channel before execution, applying
known external forces and measuring compliance. This change increases
median standardized signal by 11.46x on fresh synthetic truth worlds, yet still
does not cross the fixed decision magnitude.

The resulting contribution is not a claim that DCS or force compliance
is a solved prospective verifier. Instead, this work contributes:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\item
  \textbf{Deceptive physical repair.} We formalize and experimentally
  instantiate the failure mode in which ordinary held-out fit improves
  while an untouched physical target worsens.
\item
  \textbf{Reality Probe.} We develop an evidence-governed
  rewrite-verification architecture that separates proposal generation
  from confirmatory physical intervention, carries explicit measurement,
  repeat, and numerical uncertainty, and preserves support, veto, or
  unresolved outcomes rather than forcing a binary verdict.
\item
  \textbf{Prospective falsification of the stronger DCS hypothesis.} We show that
  lower-order-annihilating intervention contrasts can succeed on a
  constructed control yet fail to provide useful prospective
  discrimination in frozen realistic regimes; this negative result is
  retained rather than retuned away.
\item
  \textbf{Orthogonal physical-evidence experiment.} On fresh truth
  worlds, changing the physical interrogation from kinematic
  counterfactuals to known-force compliance raises median standardized
  evidence by 11.46$\times$ under the unchanged decision rule, while
  still remaining insufficient for certification.
\end{enumerate}

The central conclusion is therefore about evidence, not optimism: a
captured world can look better and still be physically worse, and a
verifier should remain unresolved when the available experiment does not
contain enough discriminating information.

\section{Related Work}\label{related-work}

\subsection{Physics-aware scene representations and inverse
physics}\label{physics-aware-scene-representations-and-inverse-physics}

PAC-NeRF couples continuum simulation to a neural radiance field for
joint geometry and physical-parameter estimation from multi-view video
\citep{li2023pacnerf}. PhysGaussian integrates Material Point Method
(MPM) dynamics directly with 3D Gaussian primitives \citep{xie2024physgaussian};
subsequent work has extended physics-aware visual representations toward
material initialization and refinement, interaction synthesis, and
monocular or multi-material inverse physics
\citep{zhang2024physdreamer,liu2025physflow,rho2026monophysics,wada2026mphygs}. These methods establish
that high-quality visual representations can support physical inference
and simulation. Reality Probe does not claim novelty for
Gaussian-plus-MPM simulation, parameter fitting, or differentiable
inverse physics. We instead ask whether a proposed physical rewrite is
independently supported once an optimizer has already produced an
observational improvement.

Differentiable simulation systems such as ChainQueen and DiffTaichi
provide important infrastructure for gradient-based physical
optimization \citep{hu2019chainqueen,hu2020difftaichi}. In our implementation, finite
differences, adjoint sensitivity, APIC/MPM replay, and material
calibration are enabling components rather than contributions by
themselves.

\subsection{Identifiability and experimental
design}\label{identifiability-and-experimental-design}

Physical inference can be limited by the information content of the
observed trajectories. Recent work provides structural identifiability
conditions directly from video for classes of dynamical systems
\citep{wang2026identifiability}, while measurement-grounded benchmarks such as IRIS
evaluate parameter accuracy, identifiability, extrapolation, and
governing-equation recovery on controlled real videos \citep{khanbayov2026iris}. In mechanics, optimal experiment design uses sensitivity,
Jacobian conditioning, and Fisher-information concepts to choose
informative loading conditions \citep{asadi2025oed}. These ideas motivate our matched baselines and the distinction between
weak and informative interventions. Classical model-discrimination and
optimal-experiment-design reasoning likewise asks which excitation best
separates competing explanations; Reality Probe does not claim that
principle as new. Our narrower contribution is to make evidence
sufficiency an explicit transaction rule for a requested captured-world
rewrite, to retain unresolved outcomes, and to measure what changes when
the physical evidence channel itself is changed.

\subsection{Simulation credibility and measured physical
fidelity}\label{simulation-credibility-and-measured-physical-fidelity}

Verification, validation, uncertainty quantification, and intended-use
credibility have long been central to computational modeling \citep{oberkampf2007pcmm}. More recent digital-twin work explicitly
considers validation of simulated counterfactuals \citep{laudy2026digitaltwin}. These
frameworks make broad credibility questions explicit, but they do not
remove the application-specific need to determine whether a particular
physical edit is supported by the available experiment.

GAUGE provides a recent measurement-grounded benchmark for physical
fidelity across simulation engines and video world models, covering
rigid, cable, textile, and volumetric-deformation tasks \citep{wang2026gauge}. We use a public GAUGE foam-shearing subset only for a
retrospective channel-contradiction analysis. That analysis is
intentionally not presented as fresh prospective confirmation.

\subsection{Measured deformable
tracking}\label{measured-deformable-tracking}

For engineering validation of the captured-world pipeline, we also use
sequence C2 from the DOT deformable tracking dataset, which provides
measured 3D correspondences obtained using invisible markers
\citep{li2025dot}. DOT supplies geometry and motion measurements, but not the
stress-free state, loads, thickness, mass, or constitutive ground truth
required to identify the true cloth material. We therefore treat the
inferred parameters in that benchmark as model-conditioned effective
quantities and keep this measured-source system check separate from the
main verification claim.

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{problem-formulation}

Let \(M\) denote an executable captured world and let \(R_{0}\) and
\(R_{1}\) denote a baseline and a proposed repaired physical
realization. We distinguish three data roles: fitting evidence used to
calibrate or propose a rewrite, ordinary held-out evidence used to
measure generalization within the same observation family, and an
untouched target or independent physical intervention used only after
the repair is proposed.

Let \(L_{obs}(R)\) be the ordinary held-out loss and \(L_{target}(R)\)
be an independent physical target. We define a deceptive repair by

\[L_{obs}\left( R_{1} \right) < L_{obs}\left( R_{0} \right)\quad\text{and}\quad L_{target}\left( R_{1} \right) > L_{target}\left( R_{0} \right).\]

This definition is deliberately agnostic to why the mismatch occurs.
Model-form inadequacy, parameter compensation, transfer choice, boundary
assumptions, and numerical artifacts can all create observationally
plausible but physically different worlds.

A verification channel produces an error \(e(R,Y)\) against independent
evidence \(Y\) and an uncertainty scale \(\sigma\). We use the signed
progress statistic

\[z = \frac{e\left( R_{0},Y \right) - e\left( R_{1},Y \right)}{\sigma}.\]

The frozen decision rule is

\[z \geq 2 \Rightarrow \text{support},\quad\quad z \leq - 2 \Rightarrow \text{veto},\quad\quad|z| < 2 \Rightarrow \text{unresolved}.\]

The magnitude 2 is an operational decision reference fixed before the
fresh evaluation; it is not claimed to be a universal physical constant.
The distinction matters because the main experiments remain unresolved
rather than being reclassified after inspection.

\section{Reality Probe}\label{reality-probe}

\subsection{Captured-world rewrite and verification
pipeline}\label{captured-world-rewrite-and-verification-pipeline}

Reality Probe separates proposal generation from verification. A
captured bundle provides appearance-linked state, particle state,
observations, uncertainty sidecars, and provenance. The implementation
calibrates a candidate physical model using the fitting split, evaluates
ordinary held-out error, proposes a local or model-level rewrite, and
only then evaluates independent counterfactual evidence. If the
verification channel produces support, the system may commit the
rewrite; if it produces veto, the rewrite is rejected; if evidence is
unresolved, the system refuses certification rather than forcing a
binary answer.


The evidence layer deliberately has three outcomes, while the lower-level
world transaction has two terminal states: committed or not committed. In the
implementation, support can lead to a verified commit; both veto and unresolved
map to a rejected/non-commit transaction, and any tentative mutation is rolled
back. The three-way scientific distinction is retained in the evidence record
even though the storage transaction is binary.

The verification transaction is deliberately sequential: proposal
generation is completed before the independent physical probe is
evaluated, and the resulting evidence may support, veto, or leave the
rewrite unresolved. This ordering prevents the confirmatory channel from
silently becoming part of the proposal objective.

The research implementation is in C++20 and uses nonlinear MPM replay,
APIC/PIC transfer variants, finite-difference and adjoint influence
paths, deterministic experiment drivers, frozen JSON/CSV result ledgers,
and SHA-256-indexed evidence packaging. The paper does not depend on
native rendering for its quantitative conclusions; Metal and Vulkan
render paths are used for system integration and presentation.

\subsection{Dark-Field Counterfactual
Spectroscopy}\label{dark-field-counterfactual-spectroscopy}

For a model \(M\) and physical intervention \(u\), let \(F_{M}(u)\)
denote an observable response vector. DCS applies a signed intervention
measure \(\mu\) to form

\[\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\left\lbrack F_{M} \right\rbrack = \sum_{i}^{}w_{i}F_{M}\left( u_{i} \right).\]

For a declared mechanism order \(k\), the stencil weights are required
to annihilate all lower-order multivariate monomials,

\[r_{\alpha} = \sum_{i}^{}w_{i}u_{i}^{\alpha} \approx 0,\quad\quad|\alpha| < k.\]

The implementation enumerates these monomials and validates the maximum
residual against a tolerance rather than hard-coding only one stencil. A
familiar order-2 one-dimensional example is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:second}
F(+a)+F(-a)-2F(0).
\end{equation}

which cancels constant and linear response. In the experiments, however,
the order-2 comparator is synthesized on a \(3 \times 3\) intervention
lattice; Eq.~\ref{eq:second} is explanatory rather than the literal
selected stencil.

The system also implements finite-amplitude Boolean-lattice interactions
through the Möbius contrast

\[\kappa(S) = \sum_{T \subseteq S}^{}( - 1)^{|S| - |T|}F(T).\]

We treat this as an interaction statistic, not as a newly derived
mixed-derivative theorem.

\subsection{Uncertainty and standardized
separation}\label{uncertainty-and-standardized-separation}

The evidence denominator separates measurement, repeat, and numerical
uncertainty. Independent measurement and repeat variance propagate
through a signed stencil with the squared \(\ell_{2}\) weight gain
\(\sum_{i}^{}w_{i}^{2}\). Numerical error is treated more conservatively
because discretization error can be correlated across intervention
points. D3 additionally estimates numerical uncertainty directly in
witness space by evaluating the same signed witness at nominal and
refined solver resolution.

For candidate pairs, the verifier evaluates worst-case standardized
separation over the retained response bundle. Active stencil synthesis
first projects candidate disagreement into the nullspace of the
lower-moment constraints and then searches deterministic projected
directions. This is a heuristic for a non-convex maximin problem; the
implementation does not provide a global-optimality certificate.

\subsection{Known-force compliance as orthogonal
information}\label{known-force-compliance-as-orthogonal-information}

After D4V, we freeze a separate experiment to test whether the low
evidence magnitude is specific to the DCS construction or reflects a
broader shortage of informative physical observations. The new channel
leaves the fitting and repair-proposal path unchanged and applies a
known force only after a candidate repair has been selected.

Six fresh synthetic truth worlds use APIC transfer with Young's modulus in
\(\{ 14125,16625,18125\}\) Pa and Poisson ratio in \(\{ 0.22,0.32\}\),
with truth timestep \(2.5 \times 10^{- 5}\) s. Candidate families are
fixed before execution. The bottom particle layer is kinematically
fixed, gravity is zero, and each top-layer particle receives 40 N in
each of four experiments: \(+ x\), \(- x\), \(+ y\), and \(+ z\). For
every direction we record the mean top-layer and interior-layer
displacement in three axes, yielding a six-component response per force
experiment.

Numerical uncertainty is the RMS difference between nominal and
half-timestep candidate response bundles. Measurement and repeat scales
are each frozen at \(2 \times 10^{- 5}\) m per component. The force
progress statistic is

\[z_{force} = \frac{e(B,Y) - e(R,Y)}{\sqrt{\sigma_{meas}^{2} + \sigma_{repeat}^{2} + \sigma_{num,B}^{2} + \sigma_{num,R}^{2}}}.\]

The same decision magnitude of 2 is used for the DCS comparator and
force channel. Force amplitude, directions, truth worlds, candidate
grid, and threshold are frozen before execution and are not retuned
after observing the results.

\subsection{D1: constructed positive
control}\label{d1-constructed-positive-control}

D1 verifies the execution path under deliberately constructed deceptive
cases. All 64 proposed repairs improve the ordinary criterion and are
deceptive by construction. DCS rejects all 64. Median ordinary
observation improvement is 60.88\%, and the median witness-degradation
ratio is 9.375. We use this stage only as an implementation control; it
does not establish prospective generalization.

\subsection{D2: active selection and frozen
validation}\label{d2-active-selection-and-frozen-validation}

An active-selection discovery comparison evaluates how well different
witnesses rank target behavior. On D2b, ranking agreement is 95.83\% for
raw maximin, 87.50\% for Fisher/Jacobian selection, 75.00\% for the
same-cost raw bundle, 66.67\% for maximum motion, 62.50\% for DCS
order-2 maximin, and 45.83\% for random selection. DCS therefore does
not dominate simpler matched baselines.

The frozen D2 validation uses 16 truth worlds. No world reaches the
decision magnitude. Median standardized separation is 0.041578, median
numerical RMS is \(6.469 \times 10^{- 6}\) m, and the maximum moment
residual is \(3.886 \times 10^{- 16}\). The stencil algebra is therefore
numerically well satisfied while the physical evidence remains weak.

\subsection{D3: witness-space refinement and adaptive
order}\label{d3-witness-space-refinement-and-adaptive-order}

D3 tests whether direct witness-space numerical treatment or higher
mechanism order recovers useful separation. Six fresh synthetic truth worlds are
evaluated. Adaptive selection chooses order 2 in all six cases and never
chooses order 3. Median adaptive separation is 0.0588583. Refinement
lowers the median numerical RMS from \(8.435 \times 10^{- 7}\) m for
order 2 to \(2.153 \times 10^{- 7}\) m for order 3, yet no truth world
is resolved. Ranking agreement remains modest: Fisher 61.11\%, raw
bundle 58.33\%, maximum motion 58.33\%, pair-aware raw 55.56\%, adaptive
DCS 52.78\%, and order-3 DCS 47.22\%.

This stage separates two issues that are often conflated: numerical
witness fidelity can improve without making the experiment more
informative about the competing physical worlds.

\subsection{Measured-source engineering
check}\label{measured-source-engineering-check}

The end-to-end system is also exercised on measured DOT C2 trajectories
\citep{li2025dot}. The importer keeps measured 3D correspondences
separate from explicit model proxies for rest state, density, thickness,
mass, Gaussian photometry, and uncertainty. Fit-only calibration and
held-out replay produce a model-conditioned effective material setting
rather than a claim of true cloth-property recovery. A proposed local
material rewrite is rejected and rolled back because the complete
independent derivative-verification contract is not met, despite
satisfying the configured nonlinear linearization-error tolerance. This
result validates the commit/refusal mechanism on measured-source data
but is not used as evidence for the central deceptive-repair claim.

\section{Results and Analysis}\label{results-and-analysis}

\subsection{Deceptive repair is a distinct failure
mode}\label{deceptive-repair-is-a-distinct-failure-mode}

The D4V and fresh synthetic OFC populations establish that lower ordinary held-out
error does not uniquely determine whether the untouched physical target
improves. In D4V, 14 of 36 ordinary-improving repairs are deceptive. In
the fresh synthetic OFC population, 12 of 36 are deceptive. These counts are not
intended as estimates of a universal deception rate; they establish that
the failure mode occurs under controlled candidate-generation pipelines
and must therefore be considered by any verification rule that approves
physical edits from observation fit alone.

\subsection{The stronger DCS hypothesis is not supported in the tested
regime}\label{the-dcs-hypothesis-is-falsified-as-a-general-prospective-verifier}

The constructed D1 control demonstrates that the implementation can
produce a strong dark-field rejection when the experiment is engineered
to contain that distinction. The subsequent stages do not preserve that
strength. D2 and D3 remain far below the decision magnitude, DCS does
not dominate matched ranking baselines, and D4V provides zero resolved
coverage. These negative results are central rather than incidental:
they rule out the stronger interpretation that mechanism-selective
cancellation alone is sufficient for repair certification in the tested
regime.

The D3 numerical refinement result is particularly informative. Reducing
the witness-space numerical floor by several-fold does not materially
increase standardized separation. The limiting factor is therefore not
simply solver discretization error in the original witness evaluation.

\subsection{Orthogonal physical information changes the evidence
scale}\label{orthogonal-physical-information-changes-the-evidence-scale}

\begin{figure}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../docs/readme_assets/10_signal_gain.png}
\caption{Fresh known-force compliance raises the median standardized

The information-limit result below summarizes the frozen evidence scale.
The known-force compliance channel moves the median from the same order
as the frozen D2/D3/DCS evidence to 0.5586. This is an order-of-magnitude change in the observed standardized
evidence scale under a genuinely different physical interrogation, not
a threshold adjustment or a new score applied to the same observations.
We interpret this as strong evidence that observability depends on the
experiment, while avoiding the stronger claim that information content
is the unique limiting cause.

This result supports the interpretation that verification quality
depends strongly on which physical question is asked. Kinematic
observations that are adequate for proposing a plausible repair need not
be adequate for discriminating that repair from alternatives. A
force-controlled experiment adds information that is not available to
the fitting objective.

\subsection{Stronger evidence is not sufficient
evidence}\label{stronger-evidence-is-not-sufficient-evidence}

The strongest result in the fresh synthetic force population is \(|z| = 1.31911\),
still below the decision magnitude. The selected visual example has
signed force progress \(z = - 1.31811\): evidence moves substantially in
the veto direction but remains unresolved. The system therefore refuses
certification. We view this behavior as preferable to lowering the
threshold after observing the partition or interpreting an intermediate
score as a definitive physical verdict.

The information-frontier view also avoids a misleading binary narrative.
The force channel is not equivalent to DCS simply because both return
0\% resolved coverage. Their evidence magnitudes are materially
different, and the remaining gap to the decision rule is correspondingly
different. The experiment therefore supplies a quantitative direction
for future sensor and intervention design even though it does not
support deployment-level verification.

\section{Discussion}\label{discussion}

\subsection{What is established}\label{what-is-established}

The experiments support three linked conclusions. First, observationally
improving physical repairs can be deceptive with respect to an untouched
target. Second, mechanism-sensitive verification signals can remain too
weak to support a reliable repair decision even when their numerical
implementation is stable. Third, changing to a genuinely different
physical channel can substantially increase observability without
necessarily making the evidence sufficient for certification.

These conclusions are narrower than a universal physical-correctness
claim, but they are operational. A persistent captured-world system can
preserve the distinction between fitting, verification, and unresolved
evidence, and it can refuse a requested rewrite without treating refusal
as a pipeline failure.

\subsection{Relation to identifiability and experiment
design}\label{relation-to-identifiability-and-experiment-design}

The force-compliance result is consistent with the broader principle
that parameter and model discrimination depend on excitation and
measurement design \citep{asadi2025oed,wang2026identifiability}. Our
experiments do not introduce that principle. Their contribution is to
expose its consequence inside a physical-rewrite workflow: verification
can fail not because a candidate looks implausible, but because the
available experiment does not separate plausible candidates strongly
enough. This distinction changes the system response from ``pick the
best score'' to ``request more informative evidence or remain
unresolved.''

\subsection{Relation to simulation verification and digital
twins}\label{relation-to-simulation-verification-and-digital-twins}

Simulation credibility frameworks already separate code verification,
solution verification, model validation, and uncertainty \citep{oberkampf2007pcmm}. Digital-twin validation work similarly
emphasizes which counterfactual claims are supported by observable
validation data \citep{laudy2026digitaltwin}. Reality Probe should therefore be read as
an application-specific mechanism rather than a replacement for those
frameworks. Its scope is a physically editable captured world where a
candidate rewrite is proposed and an independent intervention is used to
decide whether the rewrite should be committed.

\subsection{Limitations}\label{limitations}

The main limitations are substantive.

\begin{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{Fresh prospective force evidence is synthetic.} The orthogonal
  force-compliance experiment uses controlled MPM truth worlds rather
  than a measured force-sensor setup. A real prospective force
  experiment is required for a stronger measured-world claim.
\item
  \textbf{GAUGE is retrospective.} The measured channel contradiction
  motivates the problem and demonstrates that different observables can
  disagree, but it is not an untouched prospective test of DCS or force
  compliance.
\item
  \textbf{The physical regime is limited.} The principal experiments use
  a fixed deformable MPM setup and selected APIC/PIC candidate families.
  Generalization across constitutive families, contact regimes, and
  solvers is not established.
\item
  \textbf{The decision magnitude is operational.} The value 2 is frozen
  before evaluation and prevents post-hoc threshold tuning, but it is
  not a universal law for all physical verification tasks. A post-hoc
  robustness audit uses the frozen per-proposal ledger: force compliance
  has 14/36 crossings at $\tau=1$ (6 support and 8 veto), while both
  fresh channels have zero crossings for every $\tau\ge1.5$.
\item
  \textbf{DCS synthesis is heuristic.} Lower-moment annihilation is
  exact up to tolerance, but the projected maximin search is not
  globally optimal.
\item
  \textbf{The response-jet interpretation is operational.} The
  implementation estimates order of contact from validated witnesses; it
  does not reconstruct a complete symbolic Taylor tensor.
\item
  \textbf{The selected hero case is post hoc.} It is chosen
  deterministically for explanation and does not influence thresholds,
  aggregate statistics, or the standardized-evidence result.
\item
  \textbf{Spatial residuals are explanatory.} Solver-driven residual
  fields localize response disagreement but are not themselves the DCS
  decision statistic.
\item
  \textbf{Measured DOT parameters are model-conditioned.} The DOT
  benchmark lacks stress-free state, loads, thickness, mass, and
  constitutive ground truth for C2; fitted material values are therefore
  not presented as true cloth properties.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Future work}\label{future-work}

The most direct next step is not another retuning of the frozen DCS or
force partitions. It is a separately frozen measured experiment with an
independent force sensor or another calibrated excitation that
materially changes the available physical information. Modal or
frequency-domain excitation, richer constitutive families, and
intervention-specific sensor design are plausible directions. Any such
experiment should keep proposal generation blind to the confirmatory
channel and should preserve a predeclared support/veto/unresolved rule.

Notes}\label{reproducibility-and-implementation-notes}

a9da8c0aa868. The one-command runner builds and tests the code, executes

The core DCS implementation is separated from experiment drivers and

\paragraph{Generative-AI-assisted research workflow.}

not require the bunny.

\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

A physically editable captured world should not equate better
observation fit with better physics. Across controlled repair
populations, we find ordinary-improving repairs that worsen an untouched
physical target. Reality Probe provides the evidence-governed transaction around such
repairs; DCS is one tested mechanism-selective instrument inside it.
Frozen D2, D3, and D4V experiments do not support a claim that DCS is a
general prospective verifier. A separately
frozen known-force compliance channel increases median standardized
evidence by 11.46x on fresh synthetic truth worlds, yet still leaves every
proposal below the decision magnitude. The resulting lesson is practical: in the tested regimes, physical
verification can be constrained by the information supplied by the experiment,
and a trustworthy system should explicitly refuse certification when that
evidence is insufficient.
