\begin{abstract}
Captured scenes are starting to behave less like static reconstructions and more like executable worlds: their physical parameters are fitted, edited, and replayed. That creates a verification problem that ordinary held-out error cannot solve on its own. A rewrite can look better on the observations used to judge it and still be physically worse somewhere else. Reality Probe treats approval of that rewrite as an evidence transaction: proposal generation is separated from a distinct verification channel, and the result can be SUPPORT, VETO, or UNRESOLVED. The experimental sequence is intentionally mixed rather than positive-only. Dark-Field Counterfactual Spectroscopy (DCS), one mechanism-selective probe inside the framework, passes a constructed control but gives zero resolved coverage in several frozen synthetic regimes. Switching to known-force compliance raises median standardized evidence magnitude by 11.46$\times$, yet all 36 fresh synthetic repair proposals still remain unresolved at the frozen decision magnitude. A better-matched real-video observable does succeed on a repository-locked IRIS pendulum final set: the finite-amplitude period probe makes 90/110 controlled decisions correctly versus 40/110 for the matched small-angle baseline, including 40/50 correct SUPPORT cases, 40/50 correct VETO cases, and 10/10 correct UNRESOLVED placebo cases across 10 physical videos. The next domain does not repeat that success. A frozen IRIS free-fall validation run passes low-level quality checks on all five videos but has 111.7\% median acceleration relative error, so we retain it as a failed replication and leave its designated final split unopened. The result is not a universal physical verifier. It is a narrower systems claim: verification quality depends on the information carried by the chosen physical channel, and a trustworthy captured-world system must be able to support, veto, abstain, or retire a failing verification lane.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

A growing class of systems attaches physical state directly to visual scene representations, allowing captured objects to be simulated, edited, and tested under interactions that were never observed during capture. Physics-aware neural fields and Gaussian representations have made this practical for dynamic reconstruction, material inference, and interaction synthesis \citep{li2023pacnerf,xie2024physgaussian,zhang2024physdreamer,liu2025physflow,rho2026monophysics,wada2026mphygs}. Once a scene can be rewritten physically, however, fitting is no longer the whole problem. The harder question is what should count as evidence that a rewrite is actually better, rather than merely better at matching the observations on which it was judged.

That question is difficult because inverse physical models are often underdetermined by the observations we can collect. Different parameters, transfer schemes, boundary assumptions, numerical settings, or constitutive approximations may explain nearly the same visible motion and then disagree elsewhere. Structural identifiability, optimal experimental design, model discrepancy, and simulation credibility all address parts of this ambiguity \citep{wang2026identifiability,asadi2025oed,oberkampf2007pcmm,laudy2026digitaltwin}. We use those ideas as context rather than claiming them as new. Our narrower concern is operational: a persistent captured world has been given a requested physical rewrite, and the system must decide whether an independent experiment supports it, contradicts it, or simply fails to say enough.

We use the word \emph{deceptive} for a repair that improves an ordinary held-out observation metric while making an untouched physical target worse. The term describes the evidence pattern, not the intent of an optimizer. One frozen force-compliance case makes the issue concrete: ordinary held-out error drops by 14.93\%, yet untouched-target error rises by 9.03\%. We show that case because it is easy to inspect visually; it was chosen after the experiment and has no role in the aggregate statistics or decision thresholds.

Reality Probe is our attempt to make that approval step explicit. Repairs are proposed from the usual calibration and held-out observations, but verification is performed with controlled physical interventions that were not used to choose the repair. The verifier compares the reference and candidate responses, carries measurement, repeat, and numerical uncertainty, and is allowed to return support, veto, or unresolved. That last outcome matters. Reality Probe is the contribution-level framework because it defines the transaction between proposal and evidence, including refusal when the evidence is weak. DCS is one probe that we test inside it. DCS forms signed intervention contrasts that suppress lower-order common response so that mechanism-specific disagreement can stand out. The finite-difference and inclusion-exclusion operators themselves are classical; what we test here is whether they are useful for repair verification, and we keep the cases where they are not.

The experimental sequence was designed as a falsification chain rather than a collection of only favorable benchmarks. DCS first has to pass a constructed control. It then faces frozen truth-world validation, witness-space refinement, and a pair-specific repair-veto population. Those stages do not yield resolved prospective evidence, and matched raw, Fisher-style, and maximum-motion comparators also remain unresolved in D4V. Instead of moving the threshold, we measure the \emph{information frontier}: the distance between the evidence we actually obtained and the decision reference fixed in advance. The final experiment changes the question asked of the world itself. We freeze a known-force compliance channel before execution; on fresh synthetic truth worlds its median standardized evidence is 11.46$\times$ larger than fresh DCS, but it still does not reach the decision boundary.

We therefore do not present DCS, or force compliance, as a finished prospective verifier. The paper makes four more limited contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\item
  \textbf{Rewrite-level verification contract.} We formalize deceptive
  physical repair and turn approval of a physical rewrite into an
  explicit evidence transaction with SUPPORT, VETO, and UNRESOLVED
  outcomes.
\item
  \textbf{Information-boundary falsification ladder.} We retain the
  negative synthetic stages instead of tuning them away: DCS can be
  mathematically valid and still information-poor, while a known-force
  channel raises median standardized evidence by 11.46$\times$ without
  crossing the frozen decision boundary.
\item
  \textbf{Locked real-video confirmation.} On 10 fresh IRIS pendulum
  videos, a finite-amplitude period probe makes 90/110 controlled
  decisions correctly versus 40/110 for the matched small-angle
  baseline, with every placebo case left unresolved as intended.
\item
  \textbf{Preserved cross-domain failure.} A separately frozen IRIS
  free-fall validation set passes tracking quality on 5/5 videos but
  fails physical recovery with 111.7\% median acceleration relative
  error. We close that lane and keep its final split unopened.
\end{enumerate}

The thread running through the paper is simple: a captured world can fit its observations better and still be physically worse. When the available experiment cannot distinguish those possibilities strongly enough, unresolved is not a failure of the pipeline; it is the scientifically honest result.

\section{Related Work}\label{related-work}

\subsection{Physics-aware scene representations and inverse
physics}\label{physics-aware-scene-representations-and-inverse-physics}

PAC-NeRF couples continuum simulation to a neural radiance field for joint geometry and physical-parameter estimation from multi-view video \citep{li2023pacnerf}. PhysGaussian brings Material Point Method (MPM) dynamics directly into 3D Gaussian primitives \citep{xie2024physgaussian}, and later work has pushed physics-aware visual representations toward material initialization and refinement, interaction synthesis, and monocular or multi-material inverse physics \citep{zhang2024physdreamer,liu2025physflow,rho2026monophysics,wada2026mphygs}. These systems establish that visual representations can carry enough structure for physical inference and simulation. We do not claim Gaussian-plus-MPM simulation, parameter fitting, or differentiable inverse physics as contributions. Our question begins one step later: once an optimizer has already produced an observationally better physical rewrite, what independent evidence justifies accepting it?

Differentiable simulators such as ChainQueen and DiffTaichi provide much of the infrastructure needed for gradient-based physical optimization \citep{hu2019chainqueen,hu2020difftaichi}. Finite differences, adjoint sensitivity, APIC/MPM replay, and material calibration play the same supporting role in our implementation. They are machinery we rely on, not novelty claims.

\subsection{Identifiability and experimental
design}\label{identifiability-and-experimental-design}

The amount of physical information in an observed trajectory can be the limiting factor in inference. Recent work derives structural identifiability conditions from video for classes of dynamical systems \citep{wang2026identifiability}, while measurement-grounded benchmarks such as IRIS evaluate parameter accuracy, identifiability, extrapolation, and governing-equation recovery on controlled real videos \citep{khanbayov2026iris}. In mechanics, optimal experiment design uses sensitivity, Jacobian conditioning, and Fisher-information ideas to choose informative loading conditions \citep{asadi2025oed}. Classical model-discrimination design asks the closely related question of which excitation best separates competing explanations. We do not claim either principle as new. What Reality Probe adds is a rewrite transaction built around evidence sufficiency: unresolved is an explicit outcome, and we can ask experimentally what changes when the physical evidence channel itself changes.

\subsection{Simulation credibility and measured physical
fidelity}\label{simulation-credibility-and-measured-physical-fidelity}

Verification, validation, uncertainty quantification, and intended-use credibility have long been part of serious computational modeling \citep{oberkampf2007pcmm}. Digital-twin work has also begun to make the validation of simulated counterfactuals explicit \citep{laudy2026digitaltwin}. Those frameworks give the broader credibility vocabulary. They do not, by themselves, answer the local question faced here: whether a particular physical edit to a captured world is supported by the experiment available to verify it.

GAUGE is a recent measurement-grounded benchmark for physical fidelity across simulation engines and video world models, spanning rigid, cable, textile, and volumetric-deformation tasks \citep{wang2026gauge}. We use its public foam-shearing subset only for a retrospective channel-contradiction analysis. Because that conflict was already visible in the data, we do not treat it as fresh prospective confirmation.

\subsection{Measured deformable
tracking}\label{measured-deformable-tracking}

We also exercise the captured-world pipeline on sequence C2 from the DOT deformable tracking dataset, which provides measured 3D correspondences through invisible markers \citep{li2025dot}. DOT gives us measured geometry and motion, but it does not provide the stress-free state, loads, thickness, mass, or constitutive ground truth needed to identify a unique cloth material. We therefore report the fitted values only as model-conditioned effective parameters and keep this engineering check separate from the main verification claim.

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{problem-formulation}

Let \(M\) denote an executable captured world and \(R_{0}\), \(R_{1}\) the baseline and proposed repaired physical realizations. The experiments keep three evidence roles separate. Fitting evidence is used to calibrate or propose a rewrite. Ordinary held-out evidence checks generalization within the same observation family. An untouched target, or an independent physical intervention, is reserved until after the repair has been proposed.

Let \(L_{obs}(R)\) be the ordinary held-out loss and \(L_{target}(R)\)
be an independent physical target. We define a deceptive repair by

\[L_{obs}\left( R_{1} \right) < L_{obs}\left( R_{0} \right)\quad\text{and}\quad L_{target}\left( R_{1} \right) > L_{target}\left( R_{0} \right).\]

The definition intentionally says nothing about the cause of the mismatch. Model-form error, parameter compensation, transfer choice, boundary assumptions, and numerical artifacts can all produce worlds that look similarly plausible under the observations and diverge under another physical test.

A verification channel produces an error \(e(R,Y)\) against independent
evidence \(Y\) and an uncertainty scale \(\sigma\). We use the signed
progress statistic

\[z = \frac{e\left( R_{0},Y \right) - e\left( R_{1},Y \right)}{\sigma}.\]

The frozen decision rule is

\[z \geq 2 \Rightarrow \text{support},\quad\quad z \leq - 2 \Rightarrow \text{veto},\quad\quad|z| < 2 \Rightarrow \text{unresolved}.\]

The magnitude 2 is a decision reference fixed before the fresh evaluation, not a physical constant. We keep that distinction explicit because none of the main unresolved results is reclassified after the data are inspected.

\section{Reality Probe}\label{reality-probe}

\subsection{Captured-world rewrite and verification
pipeline}\label{captured-world-rewrite-and-verification-pipeline}

Reality Probe keeps proposal generation and verification as separate stages. A captured bundle contains appearance-linked state, particle state, observations, uncertainty sidecars, and provenance. The fitting split is used to calibrate a candidate model; ordinary held-out error is measured; a local or model-level rewrite is proposed; only then is independent counterfactual evidence evaluated. Support can permit a commit, veto rejects the rewrite, and unresolved leaves the candidate unverified. The system does not convert weak evidence into a binary answer merely because a downstream transaction wants one.


The evidence layer deliberately has three outcomes, while the lower-level
world transaction has two terminal states: committed or not committed. In the
implementation, support can lead to a verified commit; both veto and unresolved
map to a rejected/non-commit transaction, and any tentative mutation is rolled
back. The three-way scientific distinction is retained in the evidence record
even though the storage transaction is binary.

That ordering is more than bookkeeping. The proposal has to be complete before the confirmatory physical probe is evaluated. Otherwise the verification channel can quietly leak into the proposal objective and stop being independent evidence.

The research implementation is C++20. It includes nonlinear MPM replay, APIC/PIC transfer variants, finite-difference and adjoint influence paths, deterministic experiment drivers, frozen JSON/CSV result ledgers, and SHA-256-indexed evidence packages. Metal and Vulkan render paths are useful for integration and presentation, but none of the quantitative paper claims depends on native rendering.

\subsection{Dark-Field Counterfactual
Spectroscopy}\label{dark-field-counterfactual-spectroscopy}

For a model \(M\) and physical intervention \(u\), let \(F_{M}(u)\)
denote an observable response vector. DCS applies a signed intervention
measure \(\mu\) to form

\[\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\left\lbrack F_{M} \right\rbrack = \sum_{i}^{}w_{i}F_{M}\left( u_{i} \right).\]

For a declared mechanism order \(k\), the stencil weights are required
to annihilate all lower-order multivariate monomials,

\[r_{\alpha} = \sum_{i}^{}w_{i}u_{i}^{\alpha} \approx 0,\quad\quad|\alpha| < k.\]

The implementation does not assume a single hand-written stencil. It enumerates the required lower-order monomials and checks the largest annihilation residual against a tolerance. A familiar one-dimensional order-2 example is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:second}
F(+a)+F(-a)-2F(0).
\end{equation}

which removes constant and linear response. In the actual experiments the order-2 comparator is synthesized over a \(3 \times 3\) intervention lattice, so Eq.~\ref{eq:second} is only an intuition-building example, not the stencil that generated the reported results.

The system also implements finite-amplitude Boolean-lattice interactions
through the Möbius contrast

\[\kappa(S) = \sum_{T \subseteq S}^{}( - 1)^{|S| - |T|}F(T).\]

We use this quantity as a finite-amplitude interaction statistic. We do not claim it as a new mixed-derivative theorem.

\subsection{Uncertainty and standardized
separation}\label{uncertainty-and-standardized-separation}

The denominator of the standardized evidence keeps measurement, repeat, and numerical uncertainty separate. Measurement and repeat variance propagate through a signed stencil with the squared \(\ell_2\) weight gain \(\sum_i w_i^2\). Numerical error is handled more cautiously because discretization errors can be correlated across intervention points. D3 also estimates that contribution directly in witness space by evaluating the same signed witness at nominal and refined solver resolution.

For each candidate pair, the verifier looks at the worst standardized separation in the retained response bundle. Active stencil synthesis projects candidate disagreement into the nullspace of the lower-moment constraints and then searches a deterministic set of projected directions. The underlying maximin problem is non-convex, so this procedure is deliberately described as a heuristic; we do not attach a global-optimality claim to it.

\subsection{Known-force compliance as orthogonal
information}\label{known-force-compliance-as-orthogonal-information}

After D4V, we froze a separate experiment to answer a more basic question: was the weak evidence mainly a property of DCS, or were the available physical observations themselves not very discriminating? The fitting path and repair proposals were left unchanged. Only after a candidate had been selected did the new channel apply a known force and measure compliance.

The fresh set contains six APIC truth worlds. Young's modulus takes values in \(\{14125,16625,18125\}\) Pa, Poisson ratio in \(\{0.22,0.32\}\), and the truth timestep is \(2.5\times10^{-5}\) s. Candidate families were fixed before execution. The bottom particle layer is kinematically fixed, gravity is disabled, and every top-layer particle receives 40 N in four separate experiments: \(+x\), \(-x\), \(+y\), and \(+z\). For each direction we record mean top-layer and interior-layer displacement in three axes, producing a six-component response.

Numerical uncertainty is the RMS difference between nominal and
half-timestep candidate response bundles. Measurement and repeat scales
are each frozen at \(2 \times 10^{- 5}\) m per component. The force
progress statistic is

\[z_{force} = \frac{e(B,Y) - e(R,Y)}{\sqrt{\sigma_{meas}^{2} + \sigma_{repeat}^{2} + \sigma_{num,B}^{2} + \sigma_{num,R}^{2}}}.\]

The DCS comparator and force channel use the same decision magnitude of 2. Force amplitude, directions, truth worlds, candidate grid, and threshold were all fixed before execution. None was changed after seeing the result.

\subsection{D1: constructed positive
control}\label{d1-constructed-positive-control}

D1 is a deliberately constructed sanity check. All 64 proposed repairs improve the ordinary criterion and are deceptive by construction, and DCS rejects all 64. Median ordinary observation improvement is 60.88\%, while the median witness-degradation ratio is 9.375. This tells us that the execution path can express the intended rejection when the signal is built into the experiment. It does not tell us that the same behavior will generalize prospectively.

\subsection{D2: active selection and frozen
validation}\label{d2-active-selection-and-frozen-validation}

The D2b discovery comparison asks a simpler ranking question before the frozen validation. Ranking agreement is 95.83\% for raw maximin, 87.50\% for Fisher/Jacobian selection, 75.00\% for the same-cost raw bundle, 66.67\% for maximum motion, 62.50\% for DCS order-2 maximin, and 45.83\% for random selection. DCS is therefore not the strongest matched ranking method in this comparison.

Frozen D2 contains 16 truth worlds, and none reaches the decision magnitude. Median standardized separation is 0.041578, median numerical RMS is \(6.469\times10^{-6}\) m, and the maximum moment residual is \(3.886\times10^{-16}\). In other words, the stencil algebra is behaving numerically while the experiment is still not separating the physical alternatives strongly enough.

\subsection{D3: witness-space refinement and adaptive
order}\label{d3-witness-space-refinement-and-adaptive-order}

D3 asks whether cleaner witness-space numerics or a higher mechanism order recovers useful separation. It does not. Six fresh synthetic truth worlds are evaluated; adaptive selection chooses order 2 in all six and never order 3. Median adaptive separation is 0.0588583. Refinement lowers median numerical RMS from \(8.435\times10^{-7}\) m for order 2 to \(2.153\times10^{-7}\) m for order 3, but no truth world is resolved. Ranking agreement remains modest: Fisher 61.11\%, raw bundle 58.33\%, maximum motion 58.33\%, pair-aware raw 55.56\%, adaptive DCS 52.78\%, and order-3 DCS 47.22\%.

This stage is useful precisely because it separates two things that are easy to confuse. A witness can become numerically cleaner without the experiment becoming more informative about which physical world is correct.

\subsection{Locked IRIS pendulum confirmation}\label{locked-iris-pendulum-confirmation}

The synthetic sequence tells us that the channel matters, but it does not by itself give a measured-world success case. We therefore froze a separate real-video pendulum protocol before opening the designated final media. The final set contains 10 IRIS \texttt{pendulum\_90} videos, and all 10 pass the quality gate. The primary probe uses the finite-amplitude pendulum period relation; the matched diagnostic baseline uses the small-angle approximation. The tracker, candidate schedule, decision magnitude, and final media are fixed by repository state before the run.

Across the 110 controlled cases nested inside those 10 videos, the finite-amplitude probe makes 90 decisions correctly (81.82\%). The matched small-angle baseline makes 40/110 correctly (36.36\%). The primary probe is correct on 40/50 SUPPORT cases and 40/50 VETO cases, and it leaves all 10 placebo cases UNRESOLVED as intended. In paired comparison, there are 50 cases in which the primary probe is correct and the baseline is not, zero cases with the reverse pattern, and 60 ties. Those 110 rows are repeated controlled cases, not 110 independent physical experiments. The locked physical-recovery diagnostic has 16.81\% median relative error in the period-inferred pendulum length.

Nothing about the finite-amplitude pendulum equation is claimed as new. The important result is transactional: when the verification observable is physically matched to the distinction being tested, the same SUPPORT/VETO/UNRESOLVED contract can make useful decisions on fresh measured video.

\subsection{Second-domain IRIS free-fall failure}\label{second-domain-iris-free-fall-failure}

The next real-video domain does not reproduce that success. We froze a different equation-family replication on IRIS dropping-ball video. V6.6 first passes its \texttt{drop\_50} development gate with 4/5 quality-pass videos, zero implementation errors, and 8.05\% median acceleration relative error. We then open the untouched \texttt{drop\_100/06..10} validation split without changing the selector.

The validation result fails cleanly. All five videos pass the low-level quality checks, but the median acceleration relative error is 111.7\%; the frozen truth-control accuracy and direction-sign rate are both zero. Post-validation diagnosis shows that the method often treats a partial ballistic interval as if it covered the full known drop height. We then exhaust the obvious retrospective rescue families---alternate event ranking, higher resolution, longer association, track stitching, global or chunk scaling, stationary endpoint scaling, and bidirectional hold-to-hold recovery---without finding a robust target-free estimator that generalizes across the spent development and validation evidence.

That lane is therefore closed. \texttt{drop\_100} is permanently retrospective, none of the rescue diagnostics is treated as confirmatory evidence, and the designated \texttt{drop\_150} final split remains unopened. This failure is part of the paper's evidence rather than something to hide: the verification transaction only helps when the physical channel actually carries the information needed for the decision.

\section{Results and Analysis}\label{results-and-analysis}

\subsection{Deceptive repair is a distinct failure
mode}\label{deceptive-repair-is-a-distinct-failure-mode}

D4V and the fresh synthetic OFC population both show why ordinary held-out improvement is not enough. D4V contains 14 deceptive repairs among 36 ordinary-improving proposals; fresh synthetic OFC contains 12 among 36. Those fractions should not be read as a universal deception rate. They serve a more modest purpose: under controlled candidate-generation pipelines, the failure mode exists often enough that a verification rule based only on observation fit cannot ignore it.

\subsection{The stronger DCS hypothesis is not supported in the tested
regime}\label{the-dcs-hypothesis-is-falsified-as-a-general-prospective-verifier}

D1 shows that dark-field rejection can be strong when the experiment is deliberately constructed around that distinction. The strength does not survive the prospective stages. D2 and D3 stay far below the decision magnitude, DCS does not beat the matched ranking baselines, and D4V resolves none of the repair proposals. We treat that as a central result, not an embarrassing corner case. It rules out the stronger story that mechanism-selective cancellation by itself is enough for repair certification in the regime we tested.

The D3 refinement result sharpens this point. Lowering the numerical floor in witness space by several-fold barely changes standardized separation. Whatever is limiting the original witness, it is not simply coarse solver discretization.

\subsection{Orthogonal physical information changes the evidence
scale}\label{orthogonal-physical-information-changes-the-evidence-scale}

The force-compliance experiment shifts the evidence scale in a way the earlier numerical refinements did not. We take this as strong evidence that observability is experiment-dependent, while stopping short of claiming that information content is the only factor limiting verification.

This is why the choice of physical question matters. Kinematic observations may be perfectly adequate for proposing a plausible repair and still be poor at distinguishing that repair from alternatives. A force-controlled test introduces a different constraint on the candidate worlds; in these experiments, that extra constraint is visibly more informative.

\subsection{Stronger evidence is not sufficient
evidence}\label{stronger-evidence-is-not-sufficient-evidence}

The largest magnitude in the fresh synthetic force population is \(|z|=1.31911\), still below the frozen decision boundary. The visualized case has signed force progress \(z=-1.31811\): it moves strongly toward veto but does not reach it. Reality Probe therefore leaves the repair unresolved. We prefer that outcome to lowering the threshold after seeing the partition or pretending that an intermediate score is a definitive physical verdict.

The information-frontier view also prevents a misleading all-or-nothing summary. DCS and force compliance both have 0\% resolved coverage at \(\tau=2\), but they are not equally informative. Their evidence magnitudes, and therefore their remaining distance to the decision boundary, are very different. That difference gives a quantitative direction for future intervention and sensor design even though the present experiment does not justify deployment-level certification.

\section{Discussion}\label{discussion}

\subsection{What is established}\label{what-is-established}

Four conclusions survive the full sequence. First, a rewrite can improve ordinary observations while worsening an untouched physical target. Second, a mechanism-sensitive probe can be implemented correctly and still carry too little information for a reliable decision. Third, changing the interrogation can matter dramatically: known-force compliance raises the synthetic evidence scale, and the locked finite-amplitude pendulum observable produces useful SUPPORT/VETO/UNRESOLVED decisions on fresh real video. Fourth, that success is not automatically transferable. The frozen free-fall replication passes development and then fails untouched validation.

That is narrower than a claim of universal physical correctness, but it is more operationally honest. A persistent captured-world system can keep fitting, verification, and unresolved evidence separate; it can commit or veto when a matched channel supplies enough evidence, and it can abstain or retire a lane when the experiment itself is inadequate.

\subsection{Relation to identifiability and experiment
design}\label{relation-to-identifiability-and-experiment-design}

The force result fits the broader experimental-design principle that model discrimination depends on what is excited and what is measured \citep{asadi2025oed,wang2026identifiability}. We are not introducing that principle. What the experiments expose is its consequence inside a rewrite workflow: verification can fail even when every candidate looks plausible because the experiment does not separate them strongly enough. In that situation the system should not simply pick the highest score. It should collect more informative evidence or remain unresolved.

\subsection{Relation to simulation verification and digital
twins}\label{relation-to-simulation-verification-and-digital-twins}

Simulation-credibility frameworks already distinguish code verification, solution verification, model validation, and uncertainty \citep{oberkampf2007pcmm}. Digital-twin validation similarly asks which counterfactual claims are actually supported by observable validation data \citep{laudy2026digitaltwin}. Reality Probe sits underneath that broader view as an application-specific mechanism. Its scope is a captured world that can be physically edited, where a proposed rewrite is tested through a separately defined verification intervention or observable before commit.

\subsection{Limitations}\label{limitations}

The limitations are not bookkeeping details; several of them define the boundary of the claim.

\begin{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{Fresh prospective force evidence is synthetic.} The orthogonal
  force-compliance experiment uses controlled MPM truth worlds rather
  than a measured force-sensor setup. A real prospective force
  experiment is required for a stronger measured-world claim.
\item
  \textbf{GAUGE is retrospective.} The measured channel contradiction
  motivates the problem and demonstrates that different observables can
  disagree, but it is not an untouched prospective test of DCS or force
  compliance.
\item
  \textbf{The positive measured result is one physical regime.} The
  locked IRIS pendulum result contains 10 fresh videos from one
  \texttt{pendulum\_90} regime. The controlled case rows are nested inside
  those videos and cannot be treated as independent physical experiments.
\item
  \textbf{The second real-video domain fails.} The frozen IRIS free-fall
  replication passes low-level tracking quality on all five validation
  videos but misses the physical recovery target badly, with 111.7\%
  median acceleration relative error. We retain that failure, keep the
  validation split retrospective, and leave the final split unopened.
\item
  \textbf{The physical regime is limited.} The principal experiments use
  a fixed deformable MPM setup and selected APIC/PIC candidate families.
  Generalization across constitutive families, contact regimes, and
  solvers is not established.
\item
  \textbf{The decision magnitude is operational.} The value 2 is frozen
  before evaluation and prevents post-hoc threshold tuning, but it is
  not a universal law for all physical verification tasks. A post-hoc
  robustness audit uses the frozen per-proposal ledger: force compliance
  has 14/36 crossings at $\tau=1$ (6 support and 8 veto), while both
  fresh channels have zero crossings for every $\tau\ge1.5$.
\item
  \textbf{DCS synthesis is heuristic.} Lower-moment annihilation is
  exact up to tolerance, but the projected maximin search is not
  globally optimal.
\item
  \textbf{The response-jet interpretation is operational.} The
  implementation estimates order of contact from validated witnesses; it
  does not reconstruct a complete symbolic Taylor tensor.
\item
  \textbf{The selected hero case is post hoc.} It is chosen
  deterministically for explanation and does not influence thresholds,
  aggregate statistics, or the standardized-evidence result.
\item
  \textbf{Spatial residuals are explanatory.} Solver-driven residual
  fields localize response disagreement but are not themselves the DCS
  decision statistic.
\item
  \textbf{Measured DOT parameters are model-conditioned.} The DOT
  benchmark lacks stress-free state, loads, thickness, mass, and
  constitutive ground truth for C2; fitted material values are therefore
  not presented as true cloth properties.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Future work}\label{future-work}

The next useful experiment is not another tuning pass on the frozen DCS, force-compliance, or failed free-fall partitions. It is a separately frozen measured study whose physical observable is chosen before final data access and is plausibly matched to the rewrite being tested. Independent force sensing, calibrated excitation, modal or frequency-domain response, broader constitutive families, and intervention-specific sensing are natural directions. Whatever comes next should keep proposal generation blind to the confirmatory channel, preserve a SUPPORT/VETO/UNRESOLVED rule declared before the confirming measurements are examined, and leave failed domains failed instead of recycling them into confirmation.

\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

Better observation fit is not the same thing as better physics. Across the controlled repair populations, some ordinary-improving rewrites make an untouched physical target worse. Reality Probe turns that mismatch into an explicit evidence transaction, with DCS as one tested mechanism-selective probe rather than the framework itself. The negative D2/D3/D4V sequence shows that a valid probe can still be information-poor; the force-compliance experiment shows that changing the physical channel can raise the evidence scale by 11.46$\times$ without yet crossing the frozen decision boundary. The locked IRIS pendulum result provides the positive measured case: 90/110 correct controlled decisions versus 40/110 for the matched small-angle baseline across 10 fresh videos, with all 10 placebo cases correctly left unresolved. The subsequent free-fall replication then fails untouched validation and stays failed. Taken together, the evidence supports a conditional systems claim rather than a universal verifier: when a physical channel is well matched to the requested rewrite, Reality Probe can support or veto; when it is not, the right behavior is to remain unresolved or retire that verification lane.
